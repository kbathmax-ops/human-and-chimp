# Contact and positions — September 2026 update

Primary field observations of chimpanzee aggression document hitting, gripping, pulling and biting. They support including these categories of contact; they do not supply calibrated frequencies or human-versus-chimp win rates.

Source: [A case of intercommunity lethal aggression by chimpanzees in an open and dry landscape, Issa Valley, western Tanzania](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10651548/) (2023).

The cited event involved multiple attackers and chimpanzee victims. Its context differs substantially from the game's one-on-one encounter. The game therefore does not convert that event into numerical odds. Ground control, human defensive responses, transition probabilities, injury points and timing are authored assumptions.

The game depicts no exposed tissue. Small surface marks stand in for bite-related tears. Standing, clinch, takedown and ground-defense poses are distinct animation states, not a validated biomechanical simulation or instructions for safe contact with wildlife.
